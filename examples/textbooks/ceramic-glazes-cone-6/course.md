# Course: Ceramic Glazes for Cone 6 Electric Kilns

## Learner

Original request: beginner potter using cone 6 clay in an electric kiln; wants to understand glaze ingredients, common defects, and how to start testing original recipes safely.

Profile: beginner; practical fluency with mechanism depth; normal pace; medium-heavy practice through prediction, diagnosis, and test planning.

## Outcome

After this course, the learner can explain what the main glaze ingredient families do, read common surface defects as evidence, and design small cone 6 electric-kiln tests without making unsupported food-safety or material-safety claims.

## Course map

- [x] Glaze as Molten Glass - build the central model of glass former, flux, stabilizer, heat work, clay fit, and visible outcomes.
- [x] What Ingredients Do - feldspar, silica, clay, whiting, frits, colorants, opacifiers, and why recipe percentages are not the same as fired chemistry.
- [x] Reading Common Defects - crazing, shivering, pinholes, crawling, blistering, running, matte roughness, and how to decide what to change first.
- [x] Safe Cone 6 Testing - weighing, PPE, dust control, batch size, line blends, triaxial blends, test-tile labels, kiln wash, catch plates, and records.
- [x] From Recipe To Revision - adjust one variable at a time, compare fired results, and decide when to use published liner glazes or lab leach testing.
- [x] Cumulative Test Plan - design a small glaze test series for a chosen clay body and intended use.

Planned review points: quick scenario checks in each chapter, then a cumulative test-plan checkpoint after the recipe-revision chapter.

Safety and defect source basis: safety notes should stay conservative. OSHA and CDC/NIOSH materials on respirable crystalline silica support dust-control cautions, wet methods, local exhaust, avoiding dry sweeping/compressed air, and using appropriate respiratory protection when controls are not enough. FDA materials on lead/cadmium in ceramicware support avoiding unsupported food-contact claims. ASTM C738 is noted as a lab standard for lead/cadmium extraction from glazed ceramic surfaces. Digitalfire defect and glaze-chemistry pages are used as practical ceramics references for fit direction, crazing/shivering, pinholes, blisters, crawling, running, and the limits of target/limit formulas.

## Active publication

Published now: chapter 6, "Cumulative Test Plan."

Outcome: after chapter 6, the learner can design a small, safe cone 6 glaze test series for one clay body and intended use, including the test question, controlled variable, batch math, tile labels, shelf protection, dust controls, firing record, diagnosis plan, revision rule, and use decision.

Ideas developed: a test plan should narrow a wish into one answerable question; a line blend is readable when one trade changes step by step; dust, labels, shelf protection, and records are part of the test rather than admin work; attractive fired results do not support unsupported food-contact claims; target formulas and studio observations guide future tests but do not certify fit, durability, or safety.

Worked examples: convert a promising green mug-glaze idea into an exterior-only running-reduction test brief; design a five-tile feldspar-to-kaolin line blend; scale a 100-part recipe to 50 gram test cups; write before-firing and after-firing record fields; set a conservative use boundary.

Likely learner difficulty: wanting one firing to answer running, color, fit, durability, and food-contact questions at once; forgetting that changing colorants can create a separate variable; recording too little application/firing evidence; treating a glossy smooth result as a safety result.

Practice and feedback opportunities: written cumulative test plan, safety gate checklist, line-blend interpretation, batch-math check, defect-diagnosis transfer, and a 12-question cumulative practice test.

Planned next: the planned course map is complete. Future continuation should use learner progress to choose review, remediation, or advanced follow-on material such as glaze calculation, durability testing pathways, surface families, or colorant experiments.
